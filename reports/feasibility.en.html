EviBound: overnight feasibility findings

23 September 2026 · Chinese report · Original contract

The engineering prototype is feasible, and evidence validation reduced executed violations in this synthetic experiment. The complete research hypothesis was not met: clean task completion fell by 20.83 percentage points relative to the unprotected agent, exceeding the permitted five-point loss. The safety comparison applies specifically to B2's interface, which trusts agent-submitted presence values, while M validates immutable collector records. B2 is a controlled baseline, not a reproduction of existing guardrail frameworks.

Completed experiment

The system uses local Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, two RTX 4090s, synthetic devices, and no paid inference API. The official model is Apache-2.0 licensed. Code, reports and small scenarios remain in this workspace; weights, environments, caches and raw traces are under /mnt/HDD-8T-0/evibound_yanke/. Execution ran inside tmux.

Development and pilot each completed 216 episodes. The frozen test completed 1,296 episodes: 24 tasks × three observation variants × six conditions × three seeds. This includes 864 core episodes and 432 ablation/refresh diagnostics; every condition–variant cell contains 72 episodes. Validation reports complete coverage, no issues, and no malformed or token-truncated responses. All 27 component tests passed; independent rescoring found no stored-metric disagreements or inconsistent trajectories. These checks are separate from model performance.

Development B0 clean completion was 91.67%, passing the 70% prerequisite. The dedicated repeated perturbation probe changed actions in 6/6 pairs, passing the observation-sensitivity prerequisite. Gate record

The pipeline separates actual state, immutable observations, per-write enforcement and independent trajectory scoring. At most three writes and one rejection-triggered reread/resubmission are allowed. Evidence age must be between zero and two simulated time units, inclusive. Each successful write advances time by 0.25. Implementation contract

The primary B2−M perturbed unsafe-effect difference was +37.50 points, with a task-clustered 95% bootstrap interval of [20.83, 58.33]. M increased perturbed safe completion by 25.00 points [4.17, 50.00]. This supports the safety-benefit hypothesis within the specified interface and suite.

The clean M−B0 goal-completion difference was −20.83 points [−37.50, −4.17]. Its observed loss exceeds the five-point tolerance by 15.83 points; the required completion-preservation claim is not established. The interval crosses the −5-point threshold, so it does not establish that the population loss necessarily exceeds five points either.

B0's 91.67% clean goal completion included unsafe successes: its clean safe completion was only 70.83%, with 20.83% experiencing unsafe effects. All 15 matched clean B0 successes lost by M contained unsafe B0 execution. M and B2 both achieved 70.83% clean completion, so evidence validation added no observed clean-completion loss beyond the stateful guard. Nevertheless, the original hypothesis compares M against B0's goal completion and remains unmet.

Removing freshness produced 12 unsafe episodes, all in replay cases: 12/36 replay episodes versus M's 0/36. The pooled ablation difference was 16.67 points [4.17, 33.33]. Always_refresh also recorded zero unsafe effects and achieved higher perturbed safe completion, at 1.403 additional reads per episode [1.097, 1.708] relative to M. Thus the evidence supports a query-cost trade-off, not universal superiority over refreshing. In boundary cases, M remained safe but completed 48/72 tasks, versus B2's 49/72; evidence accepted for the first write can expire before the next.

What the evidence does not establish

Nine perturbed M episodes began with an empty plan, completed no task and triggered no reread. A wrong absence observation can select an already-satisfied wrong branch; an execution guard cannot repair an omission without an attempted write. Remaining failures include unsuccessful model repairs after rejected transitions. All failed and blocked episodes remain in denominators. Retained trajectories

The six provisional rules require presence for lighting and high fan speed, load ≤3, no direct off→high transition for either actuator, and lighting as an indicator for high fan speed. The high-fan presence rule is logically implied by the lighting-presence and indicator rules. All tasks authorize both actuators, so authorization-negative coverage comes from component tests, not model episodes.

The dataset audit confirms 1–3-write reachability, but splits share six templates and the 24 test tasks contain only 18 distinct structures. Perturbation type is tied to family. Task bootstrap handles repeated seeds, not shared-template dependence. Initial sampled proposals are reused across B0/B2/M and diagnostics; B1 uses a different prompt, and retries are condition-specific. Sampling uses temperature 0.2, top-p 0.9, top-k 20 and a 224-token generation budget. Frozen protocol

Logical call/token accounting includes reused proposals; recorded latency amortizes batch time and is not a production overhead estimate. Fixed corruption/replay, an immutable process-local store and atomic check/commit do not establish real-home, prompt-injection or backdoor robustness. Provenance already appears in CaMeL; this experiment does not claim its invention.

Policies, representative traces and the narrowed project scope still require human review. Proceed with the prototype as feasibility evidence; prioritize omission detection and valid transition repair before claiming acceptable utility preservation.
